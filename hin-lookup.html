A HIN serves the same purpose for a boat that a VIN serves for a car: it is the permanent identifier a builder assigns to one specific hull. Decoding happens in your browser — nothing you type is sent to us or saved.

Research This Vessel Further

A HIN can identify characteristics encoded by the manufacturer, but it does not provide a complete ownership, title, lien, accident, or registration history.

Coast Guard documented vessels

Larger vessels are sometimes documented federally rather than titled by a state. The Coast Guard's Port State Information Exchange vessel search and the National Vessel Documentation Center handle federal documentation records. Most recreational boats are not federally documented, so a documentation search will not find them.

State titling and registration

The majority of recreational boats are titled or registered by a state agency, and that agency holds the ownership record. Start from the official directory of state motor vehicle and titling services and ask about the vessel record process in that state.

Manufacturer identification codes

Builder codes are issued by the Coast Guard. Its manufacturer identification resources are the authoritative place to confirm which company a three-character MIC belongs to, including builders no longer in business.

The HIN rule itself

The format is set by federal regulation. You can read the current requirement, including what each character position means, in 33 CFR 181.25.

What Is a Hull Identification Number?

A Hull Identification Number is the permanent 12-character identifier a builder assigns to a single hull. Since November 1, 1972, boats manufactured or imported for sale in the United States have been required to carry one, and since August 1, 1984 the arrangement of those 12 characters has followed one fixed pattern: three characters of manufacturer code, five characters of serial number, two characters for the certification or manufacture date, and two characters for the model year. The number is stamped, molded, or plated onto the hull so it stays with the boat through every sale, repower, and repaint.

Where Do I Find My Boat's HIN?

Look on the starboard (right) side of the transom, in the upper corner within a couple of inches of the top of the hull or the top of the outboard bracket. On boats without a usable transom the number may sit on the aftermost starboard surface. A duplicate HIN is also required in an unexposed location — under a fitting, inside a locker, beneath the deck — specifically so an altered exterior number can be caught. Your title, registration card, and insurance policy should carry the same number; if the paperwork and the hull disagree, resolve that with your state agency before selling or donating.

What Can a HIN Tell Me?

From the number alone: which company built the hull, that builder's own serial number for it, the month and year the boat was certified or manufactured, and the model year the builder assigned. That is genuinely useful — it pins down the build date when a seller's memory is vague, and it separates a late-season hull from the next model year. What a HIN does not contain is anything about ownership, value, condition, engine hours, options, accidents, liens, or where the boat has been. Those facts live in records, not in the number.

Can a HIN Tell Me Who Owns a Boat?

No. There is no public national registry that maps a HIN to a current owner, and the HIN itself encodes nothing about ownership. Ownership sits in the title or registration file of the state where the boat is registered, or in Coast Guard documentation records for the minority of vessels that are federally documented. Access to those files is restricted, and Boats for Charity cannot identify the current owner of a vessel for you. If you need an owner identified for a legal reason — an abandoned boat, an estate, a suspected theft — the right route is the state boating agency, the titling agency, or law enforcement.

Can I Check a Boat's Title With a HIN?

You can use the HIN as the search key, but the check itself happens at an agency, not on this page. Contact the titling agency in the state where the boat is registered and ask what record request they offer; some states will confirm title status or brands to an eligible requester, and some will not. For a federally documented vessel, documentation and any recorded mortgages are handled through the National Vessel Documentation Center. Treat any site that promises a complete boat title, lien, or accident history from a HIN alone with real skepticism — nobody derives that from the number.

HIN vs VIN: What's the Difference?

Both are permanent factory identifiers, and people reasonably search for a "boat VIN lookup" when they mean a HIN. The differences matter in practice. A VIN is 17 characters, includes a check digit that can mathematically catch a typo, and is supported by a large ecosystem of vehicle history data. A HIN is 12 characters, has no check digit, and has no equivalent nationwide history database. So a HIN cannot be validated by arithmetic the way a VIN can, and a boat's past has to be researched through agencies and documents rather than a single report.

What If My Boat Doesn't Have a HIN?

Boats built before the 1972 requirement, homebuilt boats, and some imported hulls may never have had one, and on older fiberglass a molded number can weather past legibility. States handle this with an assigned or replacement identification number, usually after an inspection that verifies the hull, and that assigned number then functions as the boat's HIN on the title and registration. Ask your state boating agency about their inspection and assignment process. Never stamp a number onto a hull yourself.

Can Older Boats Have Different HIN Formats?

Yes, and this is where careless decoders go wrong. The 12-character layout this tool decodes is the format required for boats certified on or after August 1, 1984. Between 1972 and 1984 more than one arrangement was in use, and the date and model-year information did not always sit in the same positions it does now. Because the same characters can mean different things depending on which arrangement a builder used, this tool decodes the manufacturer code and serial number — which have been stable throughout — and reports the date fields as undetermined rather than guessing when the pattern does not match the current format. For a pre-1984 hull, the builder, a marine surveyor, or your state agency is the reliable source for the build date.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this HIN lookup really free?

Yes. The decoder runs entirely in your browser, there is nothing to sign up for, and your HIN is not sent to us or stored anywhere.

Can a HIN tell me who owns a boat?

No. A HIN encodes builder and build-date information only. Ownership lives in state title and registration records or, for federally documented vessels, with the U.S. Coast Guard. Those records are not public in the way vehicle records sometimes are, and Boats for Charity cannot look up an owner for you.

Does a valid-looking HIN mean the boat is legitimate?

No. A HIN that matches the required format only tells you the characters are arranged the way the regulation describes. It does not verify that the hull, the plate, or the paperwork is genuine. If something looks altered or re-stamped, ask your state boating agency or law enforcement to inspect it.

Why does my HIN have fewer or more than 12 characters?

Since November 1, 1972 a HIN has been 12 characters. Shorter or longer numbers are usually a state-assigned number, a registration number, an engine or trailer serial number, or a misread character. Homebuilt boats and boats built before 1972 may have no HIN at all.

What do the letters I, O and Q mean in a HIN?

They should not appear. The regulation excludes I, O and Q from the serial portion because they are easily confused with the numerals 1 and 0. If you decoded one of those letters, look again at the stamping.

Can I use a HIN to check a boat's title or accident history?

Not from the number itself. You use the HIN as the search key when you request records from the state agency that titles or registers the boat, or when you search U.S. Coast Guard documentation records. This page links to those official starting points.

Do I need a HIN to donate a boat?

It helps, because the HIN is how a specific hull is identified on paperwork. Boats with a missing HIN, a missing title, or an estate situation are still reviewed case by case, so tell us what you have and we will explain the next step.
