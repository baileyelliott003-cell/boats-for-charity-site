Free Boat HIN Lookup & Hull Identification Number Decoder

Enter a 12-character Hull Identification Number (HIN) to decode information contained in the number and find official resources for researching your boat.

A HIN is usually found on the starboard side of the transom. Spaces and dashes are ignored.

A HIN serves the same purpose for a boat that a VIN serves for a car: it is the permanent identifier a builder assigns to one specific hull. Decoding happens in your browser — nothing you type is sent to us or saved.

Research This Vessel Further

A HIN can identify characteristics encoded by the manufacturer, but it does not provide a complete ownership, title, lien, accident, or registration history.

Coast Guard documented vessels

Some vessels are documented federally rather than titled by a state. The Coast Guard's Port State Information Exchange vessel search covers federally documented vessels; a state-titled or state-registered boat may not appear there.

State titling and registration

State-titled or state-registered boats are handled by the applicable state agency, although the responsible office and public-record rules vary. Use the official state motor vehicle and titling directory as a starting point, then confirm the boat-record office for that state.

Manufacturer identification codes

Builder codes are issued by the Coast Guard. Its manufacturer identification resources are the authoritative place to confirm which company a three-character MIC belongs to, including builders no longer in business.

The HIN rule itself

The format is set by federal regulation. You can read the current requirement, including what each character position means, in 33 CFR 181.25.
