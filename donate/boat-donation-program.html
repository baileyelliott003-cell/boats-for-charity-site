Why Donating Your Boat is Smarter Than Selling

Selling a used watercraft privately often turns into months of wasted time: fielding lowball offers, paying ongoing marina slip fees, renewing insurance, and arranging test drives for unvetted buyers. When you donate through Boats for Charity, you bypass the headache entirely.

As an Oregon-based 501(c)(3) nonprofit (EIN 41-2487552) serving donors nationwide, our mission is to turn unused watercraft into resources that directly benefit orphanages, community programs, and veteran initiatives.

Types of Vessels We Accept

Whether your boat is in turnkey condition or has been in dry storage for years, we review each vessel individually:

  • Powerboats & Cruisers: Bowriders, center consoles, express cruisers, cuddy cabins, and pontoon boats.
  • Sailboats & Catamarans: Day sailors, coastal cruisers, bluewater yachts, and multihulls.
  • Luxury Yachts & Commercial Craft: Motor yachts, sportfishing vessels, trawlers, and USCG-documented craft.
  • Personal Watercraft (PWCs): Jet skis, Sea-Doos, and WaveRunners (with or without trailers).
  • Boat Trailers: Single, tandem, or triple-axle marine trailers.

Frequently Asked Questions About Donating a Boat

Can I donate a boat that does not run?

Yes. Many non-running boats still hold substantial salvage, rebuild, or parts value. We review condition on a case-by-case basis.

What if I don't have a clear title in hand?

Title requirements vary by state. While a clear title is preferred, our specialists can frequently guide you through state duplicate title or release of interest paperwork.

How much can I deduct on my taxes?

Under IRS regulations, if your donated vessel sells for more than $500, your deduction is generally equal to the exact gross sale price, reported on IRS Form 1098-C provided by our organization.
