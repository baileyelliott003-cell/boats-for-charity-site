Biscayne Bay, High-and-Dry Racks, and Saltwater Wear

Miami presents a distinct boating environment. Due to intense real estate density along the Atlantic and Intracoastal Waterway, a vast percentage of local sportfishers, center consoles, and dual-consoles are stored in multi-tier forklift dry-stack barns. While dry storage shields hulls from bottom fouling and barnacle growth, unused engines frequently fall victim to fuel separation, gummed injectors, and drained marine battery banks under sub-tropical humidity.

For vessels moored in wet slips along the Miami River or Venetian Islands, aggressive tidal currents and warm saltwater rapidly deplete sacrificial zinc anodes and degrade cooling passages in inboard and outboard motors. When family schedules change or a vessel sits idle across multiple seasons, continuing to carry slip fees, mandatory storm haul-out insurance, and periodic bottom cleanings becomes financially impractical.

Trailered Center Consoles

Document trailer axle condition, tire tread, bunk carpet, lighting wiring, winch operation, and separate Florida DHSMV trailer registration or title.

Canal Docks & Wet Slips

Disclose vessel draft, beam, fixed bridge clearances (e.g., Venetian Causeway, Tamiami Canal bridges), tidal depth at low water, and dockmaster access policies.

Florida Title & Registration Paperwork

Florida law mandates clear title documentation before watercraft ownership can transfer. Motorized vessels require a Florida Certificate of Title (paper or electronic confirmation from the Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles). If your boat includes a manufactured trailer, Florida titles trailers weighing 2,000 lbs or more, while lighter trailers carry DHSMV registrations. For federally documented yachts, transfers proceed under USCG National Vessel Documentation Center guidelines.
