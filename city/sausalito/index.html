Boat Donation in Sausalito, California

A useful Sausalito request starts with the boat's exact Richardson Bay setting: berth, mooring, anchorage, yard, rack, or trailer.

Describe where the boat sits—not just the city

Sausalito's waterfront includes marinas, boatyards, and vessels kept on Richardson Bay. Those settings are not interchangeable. A boat behind a marina gate presents different access facts from one on a mooring, at anchor, blocked in a yard, or stored inland on a trailer. Start with the exact location and the facility or access contact, identify who may authorize boarding, and report any known access hazard.

The City of Sausalito maintains current public information about its harbors and marinas. That directory is useful for confirming a facility, but it does not establish a donation, access permission, or pickup plan. Boats for Charity reviews the facts you submit; it does not operate a local office, yard, or pickup service in Sausalito.

Condition facts shaped by the bay

For a boat kept afloat, report the last operating or sailing date, bottom-service history, bilge condition, known leaks, corrosion, growth, rigging age, battery condition, and whether dock lines or mooring gear need attention. For a hauled or trailered boat, add the blocking, trailer, tires, hubs, brakes, coupler, and route to the road. A hopeful label such as “minor work” is less useful than dates, photographs, and a direct list of defects.

Photograph the full hull, deck, cockpit, cabin, helm, engine space, bilge, rig, identification plates, and every area of known damage. If a facility or vessel-access rule prevents a photograph, say so rather than guessing. Donor-reported facts are treated as reports unless independently supported.

Berth, anchorage, yard, and trailer details

Marina berth

Give the marina, slip or berth, gate and dock procedure, balance or deadline, insurance requirement, and whether an approved vendor or prior authorization is required for access.

Mooring or anchorage

Give the precise location, lawful access method, tender status, mooring condition, applicable notices, and the person authorized to permit boarding. Do not arrange movement from an inquiry.

Yard, rack, or trailer

Describe blocking or rack position, lift requirements, gate width, vendor rules, launch availability, trailer papers and condition, and any storage deadline or unpaid balance.

California and federal ownership records

Match the owner name to the hull identification number and every available record. Depending on the vessel, that file may include California registration, U.S. Coast Guard documentation, a lien release, trailer title or registration, marina records, and estate, trust, divorce, or business authority. A marina account or possession of the keys does not by itself prove authority to transfer the vessel.

Use the boat donation paperwork guide to organize the file. Check current California requirements with the appropriate state agency and federal documentation directly with the U.S. Coast Guard National Vessel Documentation Center. Do not sign a title, release the boat, or cancel existing obligations merely because information was submitted.

Buyer access and pickup after a sale

For a boat in Sausalito, Boats for Charity does not move, tow, transport, pick up, or store the vessel. It remains with the owner during review and while an accepted boat is offered for sale. After cleared payment, the buyer coordinates pickup or transport directly with the owner and follows any marina, yard, mooring, or anchorage requirements.

Keep the boat secure and keep storage, moorage, insurance, and other owner obligations current until the authorized transfer is complete. A buyer may need to evaluate tides, draft, mechanical condition, tender access, haul-out needs, dimensions, or trailer condition, but that happens after a sale. No inquiry creates permission to board or move the boat.

Build a complete Sausalito request

  1. Identify the legal owner and any lien, estate, trust, or business authority.
  2. Record the hull number, registration or documentation number, and trailer VIN when applicable.
  3. Photograph present condition and describe the berth, anchorage, yard, rack, or trailer.
  4. Disclose balances, notices, deadlines, access restrictions, missing gear, and known damage.
  5. Keep custody and all owner obligations in place unless written transfer instructions say otherwise.

Questions from Sausalito boat owners

Can I submit a non-running boat in Sausalito?

You may submit it for individual review. State when it last ran, whether it is in a berth, at anchor, hauled, or trailered, and what is known about the hull, engine, rigging, bilge, and access.

What should I report for an anchored or moored boat?

Give the exact location, mooring arrangement, access method, registration or documentation status, and any notice, fee, deadline, or restriction affecting the vessel. Do not assume that an inquiry authorizes anyone to board or move it.

What if the California or federal paperwork is incomplete?

List exactly what exists and what is missing. California registration, federal documentation, a recorded lien, trailer papers, and estate or trust authority answer different ownership questions. The legal owner and any lien must be resolved before an authorized transfer can be completed.

Does Boats for Charity pick up boats in Sausalito?

No. Boats for Charity does not provide pickup or transportation. The buyer access section explains the owner-custody process; after a sale and cleared payment, the buyer coordinates directly with the owner.
