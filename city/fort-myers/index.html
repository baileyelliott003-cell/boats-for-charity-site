Boat Donation in Fort Myers, Florida

A lot of these conversations start after a storm, when a boat is damaged, displaced, or simply not worth the next hurricane season.

After the storm passes

Southwest Florida has taken hard hurricane hits in recent years, and plenty of boats never came back the same. Some ended up on the wrong side of a seawall, some sat half-submerged for weeks, and some are still on the trailer under a tarp because the owner hasn't decided what to do next. If that sounds like your situation, you are not alone, and an individual donation review is one option to compare before the next June-to-November season rolls around.

A review starts with the plain facts: who legally owns the boat, what condition it's in now, exactly where it sits, and whether anyone can actually get to it. None of that guarantees acceptance. Each submission is only a request for review; it is not acceptance and settles nothing about timing, value, or tax treatment.

Saltwater, sun, and storm season

Boats around here live a hard life even in a calm year. Warm year-round saltwater means constant corrosion and marine growth, and the sun is brutal on gelcoat, canvas, and wiring. Add storm exposure on top of that and a boat can go from usable to a liability in a single season. When you write up your request, note the last time it ran, whether it was hauled or prepped before any storm, and what damage is visible now.

Photos do the heavy lifting. Shoot every side of the hull, the deck, interior, helm, bilge, and engine, plus the ID plates and anything broken. Don't hide the corrosion, standing water, blistering, or storm damage. The clearer the evidence, the better the review can assess whether a workable path may exist. Our non-running boat guide and the junk boat removal guide cover the damaged and displaced cases in detail.

Where the boat sits matters

Fort Myers boats split roughly two ways: canal-and-river homes off the Caloosahatchee, and Gulf-access boats that run out toward San Carlos Bay, Pine Island Sound, and the water near Sanibel and Captiva. Access looks completely different for each, so tell us which you have.

In the water

Give the marina or dock rules, the slip spot, any depth or tide issue, how someone gets keys and access, and whether the boat can still move under its own power or needs a tow.

On a trailer

Photograph the trailer VIN, frame, tires, hubs, lights, coupler, and bunks, and show the route out of the yard. A storm-worn trailer with rotted tires changes what's possible.

On the hard or in a rack

If it's on stands, blocking, or up in a dry rack, explain the lift or forklift needs, ground conditions, gate width, and any yard deadline or vendor approval you're working against.

Title, registration, and lost paperwork

The hull and the trailer can carry separate titles, registrations, liens, and even different owners. Gather each record on its own and don't sign anything until transfer steps are confirmed. Storm displacement often means lost documents too, which is common and usually workable.

Pull together the hull identification number, the registration or official number, the owner's name, any lien, the trailer VIN, and any probate, trust, or estate authority if the boat was inherited. Verify current rules directly with the state agency or, if the boat is federally documented, the U.S. Coast Guard. The paperwork checklist walks through it step by step.

Buyer access and pickup after a sale

For a boat in Fort Myers, Boats for Charity does not move, tow, transport, pick up, or store the vessel. It remains with the owner during review and while an accepted boat is offered for sale. After cleared payment, the buyer coordinates pickup or transport directly with the owner and follows any marina or yard requirements.

Depending on condition and access, a buyer might roll out on its own trailer, need a commercial hauler or a boatyard haul-out, move under its own power, or simply stay put while another route is considered. A boat that took storm damage almost always needs a closer look before any of that is settled. In the meantime, keep your storage, insurance, and security in place. Don't cancel anything on the strength of an early inquiry.

Putting a request together

  1. Confirm the legal owner and collect whatever boat and trailer documents you still have.
  2. Take clear condition, ID, storage, trailer, and access photos.
  3. Be upfront about storm or water damage, missing gear, liens, unpaid fees, and yard deadlines.
  4. Send the exact location and answer any follow-up questions.
  5. Keep copies of every transfer, acknowledgment, and tax record.
