Why Donating Your Boat is Smarter Than Selling

Selling a used watercraft privately often turns into months of wasted time: fielding lowball offers, paying ongoing marina slip fees, renewing insurance, and arranging test drives for unvetted buyers. When you donate through Boats for Charity, you bypass the headache entirely.

As an Oregon-based 501(c)(3) nonprofit (EIN 41-2487552) serving donors nationwide, our mission is to turn unused watercraft into resources that directly benefit orphanages, community programs, and veteran initiatives.

Types of Vessels We Accept

Whether your boat is in turnkey condition or has been in dry storage for years, we review each vessel individually across all categories:

Powerboats & Cruisers

Bowriders, center consoles, express cruisers, cuddy cabins, pontoon boats, and fishing vessels.

Sailboats & Catamarans

Day sailors, coastal cruisers, bluewater cruising yachts, and multihulls with standing rigging.

Yachts & Commercial Craft

Motor yachts, sportfishers, trawlers, USCG-documented craft, and commercial workboats.

PWCs & Trailers

Jet skis, Sea-Doos, WaveRunners, and single/tandem/triple-axle marine trailers.

Common Boat Donation Questions

Can I donate a boat that does not run?

You may submit a non-running boat for review. Acceptance depends on condition, ownership, location, storage, and salvage/parts viability.

Do I need the boat title before I ask?

You can ask even if paperwork is incomplete. Tell us what title, registration, USCG documentation, or lien records you have. See our titleless boat donation guide.

How is the tax deduction calculated?

Under IRS Form 1098-C rules, if your donated boat sells for over $500, your deduction is generally the smaller of the gross sale proceeds or fair market value. Read our IRS tax deduction guide.
