A PLAN FOR YOUR BOAT, FROM FIRST CALL TO HANDOFF

Keep the memories. Pass on the boat.

Changing how you spend your time? Downsizing, upgrading or handling an estate? You do not have to wait until your boat is a project to consider donating it.

Present your boat properly

We use the specifications, condition details, service information and photos you provide to prepare an informative auction listing. Recent maintenance and useful equipment deserve to be seen.

Let us handle buyer questions

For accepted boats, our team handles the marketing and buyer communication, checking details with you when needed. We guide the sale and payment process so you are not managing a listing alone.

Know how the handoff works

After the boat sells and payment clears, the buyer coordinates pickup with you. We help clarify access and timing and provide the applicable donation documentation, including Form 1098-C when required.

Your first conversation should leave you with a clear next step.

We review the vessel, ownership paperwork, photos, location and your timeline. Tell us about a storage deadline before committing. Review does not guarantee acceptance, a sale price or a pickup date; you remain responsible for storage, insurance and other ownership costs until transfer.

A Meaningful Alternative to Selling It Yourself

If maximizing your personal cash return is your priority, a private sale or broker may be the better fit. If you prefer to make a charitable contribution and have help managing the sale process, donation is worth a conversation. We welcome well-kept boats with useful life ahead of them, as well as vessels needing work.

As an Oregon-based 501(c)(3) nonprofit (EIN 41-2487552) serving donors nationwide, our mission is to turn unused watercraft into resources that directly support charitable community programs and families in need.

Types of Vessels We Accept

Whether your boat is in turnkey condition or has been in dry storage for years, we review each vessel individually across all categories:

Powerboats & Cruisers

Bowriders, center consoles, express cruisers, cuddy cabins, pontoon boats, and fishing vessels.

Sailboats & Catamarans

Day sailors, coastal cruisers, bluewater cruising yachts, and multihulls with standing rigging.

Yachts & Commercial Craft

Motor yachts, sportfishers, trawlers, USCG-documented craft, and commercial workboats.

PWCs & Trailers

Jet skis, Sea-Doos, WaveRunners, and single/tandem/triple-axle marine trailers.

Common Boat Donation Questions

Can I donate a boat that does not run?

You may submit a non-running boat for review. Acceptance depends on condition, ownership, location, storage, and salvage/parts viability.

Do I need the boat title before I ask?

You can ask even if paperwork is incomplete. Tell us what title, registration, USCG documentation, or lien records you have. See our titleless boat donation guide.

How is the tax deduction calculated?

Under IRS Form 1098-C rules, if your donated boat sells for over $500, your deduction is generally the smaller of the gross sale proceeds or fair market value. Read our IRS tax deduction guide.
