What We Review Before Accepting a Boat

A useful boat donation review is not based on age or location alone. We look at whether the person offering the vessel can legally transfer it; the hull, engine, equipment, and trailer condition; where and how the boat is stored; whether a future buyer can reach it; and whether liens, balances, facility rules, or deadlines affect the transfer.

Ownership and paperwork

Boat title or registration, Coast Guard documentation, lien releases, estate authority, co-owner signatures, and separate trailer records all matter.

Condition and identity

Year, make, model, length, hull identification number, engine status, damage, missing parts, current photographs, and recent use help us understand the vessel.

Storage and access

Tell us whether the boat is in a slip, on a lift, blocked in a yard, on a trailer, or at a residence, plus any gate, ramp, tide, crane, haul-out, or facility requirements.

Problems do not automatically end a review. Missing paperwork, a non-running engine, weather damage, an estate, or an older trailer may be workable depending on the rest of the facts. The important thing is to disclose them early. See the detailed boat donation review guide for a complete checklist.

How to Prepare a Boat Donation

  1. Confirm who can sign. Match the legal owner to the title, registration, federal documentation, estate record, trust, company record, or other authority.
  2. Gather current facts. Record the HIN, boat and engine details, trailer VIN, known damage, missing equipment, and any lien, storage, or marina balance.
  3. Take useful photographs. Include every side of the hull, deck, interior, engine, identification plates, trailer, storage setup, and route out.
  4. Request the review. Submit the facts and answer follow-up questions. Do not sign over the vessel or cancel insurance, security, storage, or moorage yet.
  5. Follow written transfer instructions. If the boat is accepted, complete the exact ownership and release steps provided, then retain copies of the final records.

Boat Types and Conditions We Accept

Whether your boat is in turnkey running condition or has been in dry storage for years, we review each vessel individually across all major categories:

  • Powerboats & Cruisers: Bowriders, center consoles, express cruisers, cuddy cabins, and pontoon boats.
  • Sailboats & Catamarans: Day sailors, coastal cruisers, bluewater yachts, and multihulls.
  • Luxury Yachts & Commercial: Motor yachts, sportfishing vessels, trawlers, and USCG-documented craft.
  • Personal Watercraft (PWCs): Jet skis, Sea-Doos, and WaveRunners (with or without trailers).
  • Boat Trailers: Single, tandem, or triple-axle marine trailers.
  • Non-Running & Project Boats: Vessels with mechanical, electrical, or cosmetic restoration potential.

There is no honest one-size-fits-all promise. Read about non-running boat donations, yacht donations, and personal watercraft donations, then submit the real condition for review.

Owner Custody, Sale, and Tax Documentation

Boats for Charity does not provide transportation or storage. The boat remains with the owner during review and while an accepted vessel is offered for sale. After cleared payment, the buyer arranges pickup or transport directly with the owner and any marina or yard. Keep storage, insurance, security, and facility obligations current until the authorized transfer is complete.

Donation or Private Sale?

Donation is not automatically the best financial choice. A clean, marketable boat with current paperwork and easy showing access may bring you more through a private sale. Donation may make more sense when charitable impact and a guided transfer matter more than managing listings, inquiries, inspections, negotiations, and an uncertain sale timeline. Common Boat Donation Questions

Can I donate a boat that does not run?

You may submit a non-running boat for review. Acceptance depends on the complete facts, including condition, ownership, location, storage, access, and likely disposition. A submission is not acceptance.

Do I need the boat title before I ask?

You can ask even if paperwork is incomplete. Tell us exactly what title, registration, Coast Guard documentation, lien release, estate record, and trailer paperwork you have. Ownership must be resolved before a transfer can be completed.

Is pickup guaranteed?

No. Boats for Charity does not move, tow, transport, pick up, or store boats. If an accepted boat sells, its buyer coordinates pickup directly with the owner after cleared payment.

Will a boat donation qualify for a tax deduction?

A completed donation may qualify, but the result depends on the tax rules, how the boat is used or sold, the records provided, and your individual situation. We supply applicable transaction documents; consult a qualified tax professional.
