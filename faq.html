Answers Before You Donate

These are common questions owners ask while comparing donation with a private listing, repair, continued storage, or another practical option.

Can I donate a non-running boat?

You may submit a non-running boat for review. The key details are condition, location, title status, storage situation, access, and likely disposition; no single engine issue decides acceptance by itself.

Do I need a clean title?

A clean title or current documentation makes everything easier. If there is a lien, missing paperwork, or an estate issue, tell us in the form so we can review the next step.

What does it cost to ask?

There is no charge to request a review. You remain responsible for storage, moorage, insurance, security, liens, and other owner obligations until the authorized transfer is complete.

What boats do you accept?

We review powerboats, sailboats, cruisers, yachts, personal watercraft, trailers, and documented vessels. Running condition helps, but it is not the only factor.

What happens after I submit?

A coordinator follows up to confirm details and explain next steps. If accepted, our team guides the paperwork and coordinates local pickup and transfer seamlessly.

Will I receive tax paperwork?

When a donation is completed, we provide applicable donation and transaction records, including Form 1098-C when required. Confirm deduction questions with a qualified tax professional.

What helps most

Have these details ready if you can.

You do not need every answer before reaching out. More complete context makes the review more useful and reduces follow-up questions.

Boat identity

Year, make, model, length, hull material, registration, documentation, or HIN details. Not sure what your hull number means? Try the free boat HIN lookup.

Current condition

Whether it runs, when it last moved, known damage, water intrusion, trailer condition, or storage issues.

Paperwork status

Title, lien, estate, marina, or registration questions are easier to handle when they are mentioned upfront.
