Water, Storage, and Access Factors

Owners around these waters may be dealing with desert sun, reservoir levels, rural lake storage, long towing distances, dry upholstery and tires, and trailer condition.

Waterways and storage settings

Boats in New Mexico may be near Elephant Butte Lake, Navajo Lake, Ute Lake, or stored around Conchas Lake, Abiquiu Lake, Cochiti Lake, and the Rio Grande reservoirs.

Record dry-climate wear and the actual storage point

For a New Mexico boat, photograph sun-damaged upholstery, covers, tires, hoses, wiring, and any dry or cracked components instead of describing the condition only as weathered. State whether the boat remains in a reservoir slip, sits at a lake lot, or is stored many miles away. Include the last launch date, present water or land status, gate access, road surface, and trailer defects so a future buyer can judge the location accurately.

Two New Mexico Questions to Settle Early

Can I submit a boat stored near Elephant Butte Lake or Navajo Lake?

You may submit it for individual review. A boat near Elephant Butte Lake may involve different questions than one near Ute Lake or Abiquiu Lake. Give the exact marina, yard, mooring, residence, or storage address; describe whether the boat is afloat, blocked, lifted, or trailered; and disclose access limits, balances, liens, and facility deadlines.

Which New Mexico ownership records should I gather?

New Mexico owners should gather boat and trailer title or registration records, lien releases, and storage access information. See our boat donation paperwork checklist for complete transfer guidelines. Do not sign a title, cancel storage or insurance, or release the boat on the strength of an inquiry alone.
