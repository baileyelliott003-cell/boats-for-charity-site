Yacht & Large Vessel Donation: Ownership, Appraisals & Transfer Planning

Donating a motor yacht, sportfisher, cruising catamaran, or commercial craft involves higher stakes than a standard trailer boat. Here is how to navigate USCG documentation, qualified appraisals, marina contracts, and tax rules smoothly.

What Makes Yacht Donations Unique?

Large vessels (typically 28 to 60+ feet) carry distinct legal and logistical considerations that require careful handling:

1. USCG Documentation vs. State Titling

Most yachts are federally documented through the U.S. Coast Guard National Vessel Documentation Center (NVDC) rather than titled with a state DMV. Documented vessels require formal USCG Bill of Sale (Form CG-1340) execution and lien satisfaction checks.

2. Corporate, Trust & LLC Ownership

Yachts are frequently held in corporate entities, family trusts, or holding companies. Donations require resolution of corporate authority, officer signing resolutions, and clean tax entity reporting.

3. High Ongoing Slip & Moorage Costs

With monthly slip fees, liveaboard surcharges, and insurance running $800 to $2,500+ per month, donating stops mounting carrying costs while delivering a substantial charitable tax deduction.

4. Transport & Haul-Out Logistics

Yachts cannot simply be hooked up to a standard pickup truck. Moving the vessel requires licensed captains, travel-lift reservations, or specialized wide-load road transport.

Tax Deductions & Qualified Appraisals for High-Value Vessels

For yachts and high-value watercraft, the IRS applies specific reporting rules under IRS Publication 526, Publication 561, and Form 8283:

  • Gross Sales Proceeds Rule (Form 1098-C): When Boats for Charity sells an accepted yacht through our transparent public eBay auction, your allowable deduction is generally the actual gross sale proceeds. We issue official Copy B of Form 1098-C within 30 days of sale.
  • Deductions Exceeding $5,000 (Form 8283 Section B): If claiming fair market value under qualifying IRS provisions, a written qualified appraisal from an accredited marine surveyor (e.g., SAMS or NAMS certified) performed no earlier than 60 days before the donation is required.
  • No Guesswork or Artificial Inflations: Reputable 501(c)(3) charities provide real transaction documentation that complies fully with IRS substantiation standards.

Step-by-Step Yacht Donation Process

  1. Initial Fact-Finding: Submit builder, model year, length, propulsion/engine details, USCG documentation or state title records, location, and marina information.
  2. Individual Assessment: Our team evaluates the vessel's marketability, lien status, haul-out history, and storage requirements with no application fees or pressure.
  3. Owner Custody During Sale: The yacht remains safely at your marina slip, mooring ball, or boatyard while we manage the listing and public sale.
  4. Buyer-Arranged Pickup: Following cleared payment, the verified buyer coordinates captain services, transport, and facility exit directly with your marina.
  5. Tax Paperwork Issued: You receive IRS Form 1098-C documenting the completed transaction for your tax records.

Marina & Boatyard Coordination Checklist

Before transferring a yacht, ensure these facility details are organized:

  • ✔ Slip Account Current: Clear any outstanding dockage or electric balances to prevent marina possessory liens.
  • ✔ Maintain Insurance: Keep liability and hull coverage active until ownership transfer is completed.
  • ✔ Marina Notification: Notify the dockmaster that the vessel is participating in a charitable review, and confirm vendor/buyer access protocols.
  • ✔ Engine & Systems Candor: Disclose generator hours, battery bank condition, bilge pump operability, and any known water-intrusion issues upfront.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I donate a yacht with an outstanding marine loan or lien?

A recorded marine mortgage or lien must be satisfied and released before ownership can be transferred cleanly. Contact our team to discuss payoff coordination options.

What types of yachts are accepted?

We review express cruisers, motor yachts, sportfishers, flybridge sedans, trawlers, cruising catamarans, and sailing yachts. Both running and non-running vessels in need of TLC are reviewed individually.
