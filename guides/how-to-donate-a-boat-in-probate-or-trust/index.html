How to Donate a Boat in Probate, Trust, or Estate: Executor & Trustee Guide

Settling an estate or managing a trust with an inherited boat? Discover how to donate estate watercraft, eliminate ongoing slip and storage fees, satisfy probate court rules, and receive full IRS tax documentation.

Managing Inherited & Estate Vessels: The Challenges Executors Face

When settling an estate or managing trust assets, inherited boats frequently become an immediate financial drain. Between marina slip fees, dry storage rent, insurance premiums, and winterization costs, an unused vessel can quickly erode estate value. Liquidating an older vessel through traditional private sales can take 6 to 12 months.

Donating the vessel to Boats for Charity (EIN 41-2487552) provides an immediate, legally compliant solution that stops ongoing storage costs and provides valuable tax deductions for the estate or beneficiaries.

Required Documentation by Estate Legal Structure

Formal Probate Court Administration

Required: Certified copy of Letters Testamentary or Letters of Administration, Death Certificate, and the original boat title signed by the appointed Executor/Personal Representative.

Revocable Living Trust / Family Trust

Required: Certificate of Trust (or Trust Agreement signature pages) identifying the Successor Trustee, Death Certificate, and boat title signed by the Trustee on behalf of the trust.

Small Estate Affidavit / Spousal Transfer

Required: State-specific Small Estate Affidavit or surviving spouse transfer form when total estate value falls below probate court thresholds.

Step-by-Step Estate Boat Donation Process

  1. Step 1: Free Intake & Authority Review: Submit vessel details and a copy of your Letters Testamentary or Certificate of Trust. Our estate documentation team reviews state title requirements.
  2. Step 2: Elimination of Marina & Storage Liability: We schedule prompt complimentary towing directly from the marina slip, boatyard, or private property, ending recurring storage bills immediately.
  3. Step 3: IRS Form 1098-C Tax Issuance: Upon sale, Boats for Charity issues official IRS Form 1098-C in the legal name of the Estate (for Form 1041 filing) or the designated trust/heir.

Tax Deductions for Estates and Trusts (IRS Form 1041 & Form 706)

Under Internal Revenue Code (IRC) Section 642(c) and Section 170, charitable donations made by an estate or trust can provide significant tax relief:

  • Estate Income Tax (Form 1041): If the estate generates taxable income during administration, charitable contributions can offset fiduciary gross income.
  • Federal Estate Tax (Form 706): For taxable estates, the fair value of charitable gifts provides an unlimited charitable estate tax deduction.
  • Individual Beneficiary Returns: If the boat was formally distributed to an heir prior to donation, the heir claims the deduction on their personal Form 1040 Schedule A.

Frequently Asked Questions for Executors and Trustees

What if the original boat title cannot be located among the deceased's papers?

Boats for Charity assists executors in submitting state duplicate title applications accompanied by certified Letters Testamentary and the death certificate, eliminating the need to visit the state agency in person.

Does the boat have to be in running condition?

No. We evaluate vessels in any condition—including boats that have been winterized, dry-docked for years, or non-running.

How quickly can Boats for Charity remove the boat from a marina?

In most regions, our transport team can coordinate water haul-out or slip clearance within 24 to 72 hours of receiving authorized transfer documents.
