What Types of Unwanted Boats Can Be Accepted?

Even if you think your boat is "junk," it often holds significant value in mechanical components, trailers, outboard motors, or restoration potential:

  • Non-Running Vessels: Inboard, sterndrive, or outboard boats with mechanical issues. (See our non-running boat guide).
  • Trailered Powerboats: Center consoles, bowriders, cuddy cabins, and pontoon boats on trailers.
  • Sailboats & Cruisers: Vessels in wet slips or dry boatyards facing high monthly storage bills.
  • Estate & Inherited Boats: Watercraft left in storage that family members or executors need to settle quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if I don't have the title for the boat?

We can help evaluate your options depending on state regulations, registration records, or estate documents. Read our guide to donating a boat without a title.

Does donating a boat reduce my taxes?

Yes. Under IRS rules (IRS Form 1098-C guidelines), you can deduct the gross proceeds of the sale or fair market value up to $500 directly on your federal tax return.

What if the boat cannot be accepted?

If a boat is completely structurally unsound, fire-damaged, or sunken, we will provide honest guidance on local municipal derelict vessel programs and landfill disposal options. Read our junk boat removal guide.
