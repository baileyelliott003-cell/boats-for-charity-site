Title & Paperwork Guidance

Donate a Boat Without a Title: What to Check First

Learn how missing titles, registrations, liens, documented vessels, trailers, estates, and state rules affect a donation review.

The Short Answer

A boat without a physical title certificate can still be submitted for review. The path depends on why the title is missing: a lost certificate can be replaced via a duplicate application, registration-only states never issued hull titles, and USCG-documented yachts use federal documentation. Boats for Charity reviews titleless and missing-paperwork vessels individually with zero owner expense.

Common Missing Title Scenarios

1. Lost Title Certificate

You are the registered owner of record, but the physical title was misplaced. The state issuing agency (DMV, DNR, or Marine Board) can issue a duplicate or replacement title.

2. Registration-Only States

Several states (and certain vessel lengths) only issue state registration cards rather than titles. Current registration plus a signed bill of sale is sufficient.

3. Inherited Vessels

If the boat belonged to a deceased family member, title transfers through estate administration, letters testamentary, or surviving spouse affidavits. See our inherited boat guide.

4. USCG Documented Vessels

Documented boats carry an official USCG number and Certificate of Documentation rather than a state title. Transfer is completed through the Coast Guard NVDC.

Step-by-Step Title Resolution Checklist

  1. Identify the Hull Identification Number (HIN): Located on the starboard aft transom.
  2. Gather Existing Evidence: Prior registration cards, bills of sale, insurance policies, or marina contracts.
  3. Confirm Lien Status: Ensure any prior bank loans or marine liens have formal release documentation.
  4. Separate Trailer Paperwork: Boat trailers are registered independently through state tag offices.
