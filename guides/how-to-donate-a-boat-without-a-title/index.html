Donation Paperwork & Title Compliance

How to Donate a Boat Without a Title: Legal Options, Bill of Sale & State Requirements

Missing boat paperwork does not have to prevent you from donating. Discover how title laws vary by state, what documentation you can use in place of a title, and how Boats for Charity assists you at every step.

Can You Donate a Boat Without a Title? (The Short Answer)

Yes, in many cases you can donate a boat without a physical title certificate. Whether you can proceed immediately depends on your state's boating regulations, the type and length of the vessel, and the underlying reason why the title is unavailable. At Boats for Charity, our intake coordinators handle missing-title and estate situations nationwide with zero out-of-pocket costs to donors.

1. Registration-Only States vs. Titling States

Boat titling statutes vary widely across the United States. Not all states issue titles for watercraft:

  • Titling States: Most states (such as Florida, California, Ohio, Texas, and Washington) mandate titles for motorized vessels and larger sailboats.
  • Registration-Only States: States like Colorado, Connecticut, and Maine historically rely on state registration certificates rather than formal titles for certain watercraft. In these jurisdictions, current registration cards combined with a Bill of Sale are fully valid for transfer.
  • Vessel Exemptions: Many states exempt non-motorized vessels (canoes, kayaks, rowboats) or boats under 14–16 feet from titling requirements entirely.

2. Common Missing Title Scenarios

Lost or Misplaced Certificate

If you are the recorded owner on file with your state agency, obtaining a duplicate title is straightforward. We can assist you in filing the application.

Inherited / Estate Vessel

Boats inherited through a will, probate, or trust can be transferred with court documentation (Letters of Administration / Small Estate Affidavits).

Never Titled / Bill of Sale Only

Older vessels bought privately without formal title transfer can often be submitted through historical bill of sale records and HIN verification.

USCG Documented Yacht

Documented boats do not have state titles; they transfer via official Coast Guard Bill of Sale (Form CG-1340) through the NVDC.

Required Paperwork to Prove Ownership Without a Title

To establish lawful chain of custody and ownership when a title is absent, gather as many of the following records as possible:

  1. Hull Identification Number (HIN): The 12-character federal HIN stamped on the starboard aft transom of all vessels built after 1972.
  2. State Boat Registration: Current or expired registration cards from the state boating agency or DNR.
  3. Notarized Bill of Sale: Executed by the prior owner showing transfer of ownership, make, model, year, and HIN.
  4. Lien Release Letter: If a marine loan existed, official confirmation that all financial obligations have been satisfied.
  5. Separate Trailer Registration / Title: Trailers are motor vehicle assets governed by state highway departments and require separate registration paperwork.

Frequently Asked Questions About Titleless Boat Donations

What if I bought a boat but never put the title in my name?

This is known as a "jumped title." In many cases, we can help trace the prior owner of record or utilize state-specific abandoned vessel/bonded title procedures.

Does donating without a title reduce my tax deduction?

No. Your tax deduction is determined under IRS IRC Section 170 rules based on the gross sales proceeds reported on Form 1098-C or fair market value.

Can I donate a non-running boat with no title?

Yes. Non-running boats are evaluated individually. Read our non-running boat donation guide for details.
