Tax-Deductible Boat Donation: A Complete Guide for Donors

Understand how IRS vehicle donation rules work, how your tax deduction is determined, when Form 1098-C is issued, and what records you need to file with confidence.

The Fundamentals of Boat Donation Tax Deductions

Donating a vessel to a registered 501(c)(3) organization like Boats for Charity (EIN 41-2487552) allows eligible taxpayers who itemize deductions to claim a charitable tax deduction on federal income taxes. The IRS treats watercraft under the same statutory framework as motor vehicles and aircraft (IRC Section 170(f)(12)).

Before jumping into filing forms, every donor should understand the core distinction governing vehicle donations: whether your deduction is determined by gross sales proceeds or fair market value (FMV).

Gross Proceeds vs. Fair Market Value: How the IRS Calculates Your Deduction

1. The General Rule: Gross Sales Proceeds

For most donated boats that are subsequently sold by the charity, IRS law states that your deduction is limited to the exact gross sales price realized from the sale. For instance, if you donate a cruiser and the charity sells it at auction for $8,500, your allowable charitable deduction is $8,500—regardless of whether book value guides estimate it higher.

2. The $500 Minimum Rule

If the donated boat sells for $500 or less, you are allowed to claim the lesser of the boat's fair market value on the donation date or $500. This provides a clear deduction threshold even for older, project, or non-running boats that sell for modest amounts.

Key Tax Forms and Documentation Checklist

To claim your deduction smoothly, you will assemble documents across three key categories:

  • IRS Form 1098-C (Contributions of Motor Vehicles, Boats, and Airplanes): Provided by Boats for Charity within 30 days of the sale. Copy B must be attached to your federal tax return if your claimed deduction exceeds $500.
  • IRS Form 8283 (Noncash Charitable Contributions): Required for noncash gifts over $500. When your deduction is based on gross proceeds reported on Form 1098-C, you complete Section A—even if the deduction amount is over $5,000.
  • Written Contemporaneous Acknowledgment: Official confirmation of receipt provided by the nonprofit confirming donation details, date, and that no goods or services were provided in exchange for the gift.
  • Donor Ownership Records: Signed title transfer records, trailer registration, and photos documenting vessel condition at the time of donation.

Frequently Asked Tax Questions

Do I need an independent marine survey or appraisal?

If your deduction is governed by the gross proceeds reported on Form 1098-C, the IRS does not require a formal independent appraisal. This saves donors hundreds of dollars in marine surveyor fees.

Can I donate a boat that does not run?

Yes. Non-running boats, trailered runabouts, and project vessels are routinely accepted. Review our non-running boat donation guide for specific condition disclosure steps.

What if I don't have a title?

Title requirements vary significantly by state. In some states, registration-only or bill-of-sale documentation is sufficient. See our donating without a title guide or browse your state-specific regulations.
