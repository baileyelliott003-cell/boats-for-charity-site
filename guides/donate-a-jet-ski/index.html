Donate a Jet Ski: Personal Watercraft & Trailer Donation Guide

Have an underutilized Sea-Doo, Yamaha WaveRunner, or Kawasaki Jet Ski taking up valuable garage space or incurring monthly storage lot fees? Whether running, winterized, or facing a costly jet pump or engine overhaul, learn how donating your PWC works and how to claim a fair IRS Form 1098-C tax write-off in 2026.

Why Donating a PWC Makes Practical Sense

Personal watercraft deliver immense excitement on the water, but they require diligent seasonal maintenance: battery trickle charging, fuel stabilization, jet pump wear-ring replacements, winterization against freezing, and annual registration renewals. When left sitting for multiple seasons in a driveway, HOA storage compound, or lakefront dock, watercraft rapidly lose value while maintenance costs accumulate.

Selling a used PWC privately often invites endless "is this still available?" inquiries, requests for uncompensated lake test rides, and lowball cash offers. Donating through Boats for Charity (501(c)(3) nonprofit, EIN 41-2487552) allows you to bypass the private sales headache entirely and turn your watercraft into a meaningful charitable tax deduction.

PWC Types & Mechanical Factors We Review

We review personal watercraft across all major manufacturers and configurations:

1. Modern 4-Stroke PWCs

Naturally aspirated and supercharged 4-stroke models (Sea-Doo Rotax 4-TEC/ACE, Yamaha TR-1/SVHO, Kawasaki Ultra 310) command strong interest, even with high engine hours.

2. Classic 2-Stroke Crafts

Older carbureted or direct-injection 2-stroke models (Sea-Doo XP/GTI, Yamaha WaveBlaster/GP1200) retain value among lightweight DIY enthusiasts and vintage riders.

3. Stand-Up Watercraft

Sport and freestyle stand-up skis (Kawasaki SX-R 800/1500, Yamaha SuperJet) have dedicated collector and competitive racing markets.

4. PWC Trailers (Single/Double)

Road-legal galvanized or aluminum trailers (Continental, ShoreLand'r, Triton, Yacht Club) add substantial appeal when bundled with one or two watercraft.

Step-by-Step PWC Donation Checklist

  1. Confirm Hull & Trailer Titles: Locate the state Certificate of Title or registration certificate in your name. Check if your state titles PWC trailers separately.
  2. Document Machine Details: Note model year, brand (Sea-Doo, Yamaha, Kawasaki, Polaris, Honda), model designation, HIN (Hull Identification Number), and DESS key/fob presence.
  3. Evaluate Mechanical State: Disclose whether the engine cranks, battery condition, jet pump status, and whether the ski was properly winterized.
  4. Inspect Trailer Integrity: Check trailer tire tread, dry rot, wheel bearing grease, coupler size (typically 2"), and working brake/tail lights.

Operational Transparency: How Our Process Works

Our owner-custody framework guarantees security and simplicity for every donor:

  1. Owner Custody: Your PWC and trailer remain safely in your driveway, garage, or dry storage yard during review and the public charity auction.
  2. Zero Charity Fleet Haulers: Boats for Charity does not dispatch commercial tow trucks or operating fleet haulers.
  3. Verified Buyer Pickup: Once public auction payment clears in full, the winning verified buyer arranges and funds all pickup, trailering, or transport directly with you.
  4. IRS Form 1098-C Delivery: We deliver official Copy B of Form 1098-C reporting gross sales proceeds within 30 days of sale for your federal tax filing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I donate a jet ski or personal watercraft that does not run?

Yes. Both running and non-running personal watercraft (PWCs) are reviewed individually. Disclose known mechanical issues (e.g., seized engine, bad starter, electrical gremlins, worn jet pump wear ring, or stale fuel). Non-running PWCs with good hulls or bundled on roadworthy trailers frequently retain solid auction demand.

Do I need a trailer to donate a jet ski?

Not necessarily. While trailered PWCs are the easiest for buyers to transport, watercraft located on shore docking ramps, floating dry docks, or garage dollies can also be reviewed. Disclose the storage setup so buyers can plan proper transport equipment.

Can I donate a pair of jet skis on a double trailer?

Yes. Dual-craft packages on double PWC trailers are very popular. We can process both vessels and the trailer together under a single donation review, providing complete IRS Form 1098-C documentation for the full transaction.

How does transport and buyer pickup work for a jet ski?

Under our owner-custody model, the jet ski remains at your residence or garage while listed on public charity auction. Boats for Charity does not operate tow trucks. Once payment clears, the winning buyer connects directly with you to hitch up the trailer or load the craft onto their truck at their expense.
