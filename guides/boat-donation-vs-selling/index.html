Boat Donation vs. Selling: A Practical Decision Framework

Compare donation and private sale based on time, condition, storage cost, effort, uncertainty, paperwork, and tax responsibilities.

The Short Answer

There is no universal winner between donating and selling. Selling makes sense when the boat is in prime condition, has clean paperwork, and has strong buyer demand. Donating makes sense when the monthly carrying costs, effort, and uncertainty of a private sale outweigh realistic net proceeds—and when supporting a charitable cause matters to you.

Comparing Real Holding Costs

Private boat sales often take 6 to 12 months. During this period, recurring moorage, insurance, and maintenance bills quickly erode any cash proceeds:

  • Marina Dockage: $300 to $800+ monthly.
  • Insurance & Upkeep: $700 to $2,000 annually.
  • Time & Inconvenience: Coordinating sea trials and dealing with unvetted buyers.

Situations That Tip the Decision Toward Donation

  • Inherited vessels: Avoid probate delays and long-distance boatyard management. See our inherited boat guide.
  • Unused boats with mounting slip fees: Stop monthly storage expenses immediately.
  • Project & non-running boats: Skip costly repairs. See our non-running boat guide.
  • Missing title records: Review state-specific documentation options in our donating without a title guide.
