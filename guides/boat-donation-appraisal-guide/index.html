Boat Donation Appraisal Guide: IRS Form 8283, Survey Requirements & FMV Rules

Understand exactly when an independent marine survey is required, how IRS Form 8283 Section B works, and how to substantiate Fair Market Value tax deductions exceeding $5,000 for your donated vessel.

Do You Need an Appraisal to Donate Your Boat?

When donating a yacht, cruiser, sailboat, or powerboat to a registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit like Boats for Charity (EIN 41-2487552), understanding IRS appraisal guidelines prevents tax filing errors and ensures you receive the maximum legitimate tax write-off under Internal Revenue Code (IRC) Section 170.

Whether you need a formal certified appraisal depends entirely on how your charitable deduction is determined:

Standard Gross Sales Proceeds (Form 1098-C)

No appraisal required. When Boats for Charity sells the donated vessel at auction or retail, your deduction is the gross sale price reported on Form 1098-C Copy B. You do not need to hire an independent surveyor.

Fair Market Value (FMV) Claims Over $5,000

Appraisal mandatory. If you claim Fair Market Value (FMV) exceeding $5,000 under IRS qualifying exceptions, you must obtain a written appraisal from an accredited marine surveyor and submit IRS Form 8283 Section B.

When Does an IRS Fair Market Value (FMV) Exception Apply?

Under IRS Publication 526 and Publication 561, donors may deduct the full Fair Market Value rather than the gross auction proceeds only under three qualifying circumstances:

  1. Significant Intervening Use: The nonprofit makes significant operational use of the vessel in its direct programs (such as youth maritime training, search-and-rescue support, or scientific marine missions) before any subsequent liquidation.
  2. Material Improvements: The charity performs substantial mechanical, structural, or electrical overhauls that materially enhance the market value and operational life of the vessel prior to sale.
  3. Charitable Discount Sale: The vessel is sold significantly below fair market value directly to a needy individual to fulfill the organization’s charitable mission.

IRS Qualified Marine Surveyor & Appraisal Standards

The IRS maintains strict qualification standards for non-cash property appraisals to prevent inflated write-offs. A valid boat donation appraisal must satisfy the following criteria:

  • Accreditation Credentials: Performed by an independent marine surveyor accredited by recognized bodies such as the Society of Accredited Marine Surveyors (SAMS) (AMS designation) or the National Association of Marine Surveyors (NAMS) (CMS designation).
  • 60-Day Inspection Window: The physical inspection must take place no earlier than 60 days before the vessel is formally transferred to the charity.
  • Comprehensive Condition Report: The report must include detailed assessments of hull integrity, stringers, transom, propulsion systems, rigging, electronic navigation systems, safety equipment, and current comparable sales data (BUC, YachtWorld SoldBoat database).
  • Independence Requirement: The surveyor cannot be the donor, the receiving nonprofit, the broker handling the disposal, or any related party.

Completing IRS Form 8283 (Noncash Charitable Contributions)

For deductions exceeding $500, donors must attach IRS Form 8283 to their federal tax return (Form 1040 Schedule A):

  • Section A (Deductions $501 to $5,000): Details basic boat information, acquisition date, cost basis, and donation date. No appraiser signature required.
  • Section B (Deductions Exceeding $5,000): Required for FMV claims above $5,000. Must include Part III (Declaration of Appraiser with surveyor signature and TIN) and Part IV (Donee Acknowledgment signed by an authorized Boats for Charity officer).

Frequently Asked Questions About Boat Donation Appraisals

How much does a marine surveyor appraisal cost?

Marine surveys typically cost between $20 and $35 per foot depending on vessel size, location, and whether haul-out inspection is necessary. Appraisal fees are paid by the donor and cannot be deducted as part of the charitable gift, though they may qualify as miscellaneous tax preparation expenses.

Can I use online valuation guides like BUC or NADA as my appraisal?

No. The IRS explicitly states that book values, online price guides, or dealer estimates do not qualify as a written certified appraisal for contributions exceeding $5,000.

Does Boats for Charity sign IRS Form 8283 Section B?

Yes. When a qualifying vessel meets IRS FMV criteria, our authorized officers promptly execute Section B Part IV upon receipt of the qualified appraiser report and vessel transfer.
