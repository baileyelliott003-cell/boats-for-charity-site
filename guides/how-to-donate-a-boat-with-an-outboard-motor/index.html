Donating Watercraft with Outboard Engines: The Complete Overview

Whether you own an aluminum bass boat, center console, skiff, pontoon, or inflatable RIB, your outboard motor represents a significant portion of your vessel’s overall asset value. When donating to Boats for Charity (EIN 41-2487552), your boat, trailer, and outboard motor are treated as an integrated package that maximizes your charitable contribution while ensuring effortless, zero-cost removal.

However, because outboard motors are detachable mechanical propulsion units, state titling agencies and the IRS enforce specific documentation standards. This guide covers how to prepare your paperwork and streamline intake.

State Outboard Motor Titling Regulations: Do You Need a Separate Title?

In the United States, boat titling laws vary by state. While most states title only the vessel hull and the trailer, several states treat outboard motors as separate titled property:

States Requiring Separate Outboard Titles

States such as Missouri, Ohio, Oklahoma, South Carolina, and Texas issue standalone certificates of title for outboard motors (typically exceeding specified horsepower thresholds, such as 10 HP or 25 HP). Donors in these states should locate both the boat title and the motor title.

States Using Bill of Sale & Hull Titles

In states like California, Florida, Washington, Oregon, and New York, outboard engines are considered motorized marine equipment attached to the vessel. Ownership transfers via the hull title, registration, and an itemized bill of sale listing the engine make, model, and serial number.

Acceptable Outboard Motor Types & Brands

Boats for Charity accepts watercraft equipped with all major marine propulsion systems, whether modern electronic fuel-injected (EFI) 4-strokes or classic carbureted 2-strokes:

  • Major Outboard Manufacturers: Yamaha, Mercury Marine, Honda Marine, Suzuki, Evinrude (E-TEC and classic), Johnson, Tohatsu, and Nissan Marine.
  • Horsepower Ranges: Portable kicker engines (2.5 HP – 15 HP), mid-range engines (25 HP – 115 HP), and high-output V6/V8 powerplants (150 HP – 350+ HP).
  • Mechanical Condition: We accept running motors, winterized engines, long-stored motors requiring carburetor cleaning, and non-running units with cosmetic or lower-unit wear.

Step-by-Step Outboard Donation Intake Checklist

  1. Locate the Motor Serial Number Plate: Found on the swivel bracket, transom clamp, or under the main engine cowling. Recording the exact model and serial digits speeds title matching.
  2. Document the Complete Rig: Take clear, well-lit photos of the boat hull, transom, outboard motor cowling, propeller/skeg, and trailer.
  3. Submit the Online Intake Form: Enter your vessel specifications at boatsforcharity.org/donate-a-boat or call (855) 557-3703.
  4. Sign Transfer Documentation: Our team mails or securely emails pre-filled DMV title transfer forms and bill of sale documents tailored to your state regulations.
  5. Free Coordinated Pickup: A professional transport carrier arrives to haul the entire package (boat, motor, and trailer) at zero expense to you.

Frequently Asked Questions About Donating Boats with Outboards

What if I lost the title to my outboard motor?

If you reside in a state requiring separate motor titles, our title specialists can help you file for an expedited duplicate title or prepare state-approved affidavits of lost title prior to transfer.

Does the outboard need to be running at pickup time?

No. Our towing operators are equipped with winches and trailers designed to load non-running vessels safely from your driveway, storage facility, or marina slip.

How is the tax deduction determined when a boat has an expensive outboard?

Under IRS rules, when the charity sells the complete package, your allowable tax deduction equals the total gross sales proceeds realized from the boat, motor, and trailer combined, documented on IRS Form 1098-C.
