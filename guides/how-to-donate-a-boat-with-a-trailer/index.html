How to Donate a Boat with a Trailer: DMV Titles, Dual Registration & Free Towing

Donating a boat stored on a road trailer? Learn how dual asset ownership works across state DMVs, how to handle missing trailer paperwork, and how Boats for Charity arranges 100% complimentary towing.

Understanding Dual-Asset Donations: Boat vs. Trailer Paperwork

Many donors do not realize that a boat stored on a trailer represents two distinct legal properties governed by different state agencies:

  • The Boat / Watercraft: Titled and registered through state boating authorities (e.g., State Marine Board, Department of Natural Resources, or Wildlife Commission) or documented through the USCG.
  • The Boat Trailer: Classified as a motorized road transport vehicle, titled and registered through state motor vehicle departments (DMV, BMV, or DOT).

When donating to Boats for Charity (EIN 41-2487552), our team manages both transfers simultaneously, ensuring full release of liability for both the vessel and the trailer.

What Paperwork Do You Need for a Boat and Trailer Donation?

1. Boat Title or Registration

Clear state boat certificate of title or current state registration card with Hull Identification Number (HIN).

2. Trailer Title or Registration

State trailer title, certificate of origin (MSO), or annual license plate registration document with Trailer VIN.

3. Signed Bill of Sale & Power of Attorney

Standard nonprofit release forms supplied by Boats for Charity authorizing transfer without requiring a trip to the DMV.

What If You Don't Have the Trailer Title?

It is very common for boat owners to misplace the trailer title or never receive one from a previous owner. Fortunately, missing trailer paperwork rarely prevents a successful charitable donation:

  1. Weight-Based State Exemptions: In numerous states (such as Washington, Wisconsin, and Tennessee), light-duty single-axle boat trailers under 1,500–3,000 lbs GVWR do not require separate titles and transfer via a notarized bill of sale.
  2. Duplicate Title Assistance: Where titling is mandatory, our paperwork specialists provide expedited duplicate title applications for the donor’s signature.
  3. Homemade / Kit Trailer Registrations: If your trailer was built from a kit or lacks an active VIN plate, we assist in establishing state verification pathways.

Trailer Safety & Free Towing Coordination

To ensure a smooth, zero-hassle pickup, our transport coordinators verify a few quick details about your trailer’s condition:

  • Tire Condition: Are the trailer tires holding air and safe for transport, or will flatbed towing be required?
  • Wheel Bearings & Coupler: Is the hitch coupler operational (typically 1-7/8” or 2” ball)?
  • Access & Clearance: Is the trailer accessible in a driveway, yard, or storage facility with sufficient turnaround room for a tow truck or transport rig?

Even if the trailer has flat tires, broken lights, or has been sitting for years, Boats for Charity can dispatch specialized equipment or flatbed haulers at absolutely no cost to you.

Frequently Asked Questions About Donating a Boat With a Trailer

Do I get an additional tax deduction for the trailer?

When donated as a complete package, the total gross proceeds realized from the sale of the boat and trailer are reported together on your IRS Form 1098-C, maximizing your overall charitable tax write-off.

Can I donate just the boat and keep my trailer?

Yes. If you prefer to keep your trailer or the boat is moored in a marina slip, we can arrange water-based haul-out or provide our own transport trailers.

Can I donate a trailer without a boat?

Yes. Boats for Charity accepts standalone utility and boat trailers in good condition to support our charitable operations.
