Why Donating a Commercial Vessel Makes Financial Sense

Commercial marine operators face rising regulatory compliance standards, escalating commercial slip rents ($600 to $3,000+/month), and hefty maintenance costs on heavy marine diesel powerplants (Caterpillar, Cummins, Detroit Diesel, John Deere). When a vessel reaches the end of its commercial operating cycle or a business shifts operational focus, keeping an inactive workboat berthed causes rapid financial drain.

Selling a specialized commercial boat privately can take 12 to 24 months, requiring extensive broker commissions (often 10% to 15%) and relentless negotiation over commercial survey recommendations. Donating through Boats for Charity (501(c)(3) nonprofit, EIN 41-2487552) gives your business or corporation an immediate exit with zero disposal fees, backed by official IRS Form 1098-C documentation for corporate tax write-offs under IRC Section 170.

Corporate & Legal Considerations for Commercial Donors

Commercial marine donations involve specific corporate and maritime legal procedures:

  • Corporate & LLC Authority: If titled in an LLC, corporation, or partnership, we coordinate corporate signing resolutions and authorized officer documentation for clean tax reporting.
  • USCG National Vessel Documentation Center (NVDC): Federally documented vessels require formal USCG Bills of Sale (Form CG-1340) and Form CG-1258 transfer execution.
  • Maritime Lien Clearance: Preferred ship mortgages, shipyard repair liens, or port authority fee balances must be verified and satisfied prior to ownership transfer.
  • Separation of Fisheries Permits: Federal and state commercial fishing permits do not need to be surrendered with the hull; owners can retain or independently monetize quota shares.

Operational Transparency: How Our Process Works

Our owner-custody framework guarantees complete protection for corporate and individual donors:

  1. Owner Custody: The commercial vessel remains securely at your commercial slip, shipyard, or dock while our team manages valuation and public auction listing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a corporation, LLC, or partnership donate a commercial vessel?

Yes. Businesses, LLCs, maritime corporations, and sole proprietorships can donate commercial watercraft. Corporate signing resolutions, officer authorizations, and tax EIN identification are organized during intake to ensure full compliance with IRC Section 170 corporate charitable deduction rules.

How are USCG Documented commercial vessels transferred?

Most commercial vessels (over 5 net tons) carry federal U.S. Coast Guard Certificates of Documentation. We manage the documentation transfer process through the National Vessel Documentation Center (NVDC) using formal USCG Bills of Sale (Form CG-1340) and Form CG-1258, ensuring all maritime liens are satisfied and cleared.

What if commercial fishing permits or licenses are attached?

Commercial fishing permits (state and federal fisheries licenses) do not automatically transfer with a charitable vessel donation and can be retained or sold separately by the owner. Commercial deck equipment, hydraulics, winches, generators, and electronics are documented and included in the public auction sale.

How does pickup and transport work for heavy commercial craft?

The commercial vessel remains at your commercial berth, shipyard, or port facility during review and public auction. Boats for Charity does not operate tow vessels. Once payment clears, the verified winning buyer coordinates all captained delivery, tug assistance, or commercial shipyard travel-lift haul-out directly at their expense.
