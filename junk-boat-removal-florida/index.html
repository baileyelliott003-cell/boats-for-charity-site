Operational Disclosures & How Our Review Works

To ensure total transparency, please review how our guided sale model operates:

  • Owner Custody: The boat remains safely in your possession at your dock, yard, or storage facility during evaluation and public listing.
  • No Hauling/Towing by Charity: Boats for Charity does not operate transport trucks or physical impound yards.
  • Buyer-Arranged Pickup: If the boat sells, pickup arrangements and transport expenses are the responsibility of the verified buyer after payment has cleared.
  • Individual Assessment: Acceptance, pickup availability, and timeline depend on the boat's condition, location, documentation, and buyer market demand.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Boats for Charity charge an evaluation fee in Florida?

No. You can submit the boat for a no-cost review. Acceptance, pickup availability, and timing depend on the boat's condition, location, documentation, and buyer availability.

What is the Florida FWC Vessel Turn-In Program (VTIP)?

The Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission (FWC) operates the voluntary Vessel Turn-In Program (VTIP) for eligible at-risk or derelict vessel owners who have received a law enforcement notification. Owners can surrender title to the state without facing criminal derelict vessel penalties.

How does the IRS tax deduction work if the boat is accepted?

If accepted and sold, Boats for Charity provides IRS Form 1098-C documentation. Under IRS rules, your deduction generally reflects the gross sale proceeds from the charitable auction (or fair market value up to $500 if sold under that threshold), subject to your personal tax situation.
